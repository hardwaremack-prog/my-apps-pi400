Hall 5 · Amplifiers

Amplifiers

An amp turns 100 W into 500 to 1,500 W. That's one to two and a half S-units at the other end. Worth it for DX, contests and weak paths, but fix the antenna first.

Tube amps hold lethal voltage. Plate supplies run 2,000 to 3,500 V and filter capacitors stay charged after power-off if a bleeder resistor has failed. Unplug, wait, and discharge with a grounded shorting stick before reaching inside.

Is the amp worth it?

Every 4× in power is about 6 dB, roughly one S-unit.

Power gain calculator

A good antenna upgrade (dipole to 3-element Yagi) gives 5 to 7 dB on both transmit and receive. An amp only helps transmit.

Know before you buy

US limit
1,500 W PEP output for General and Extra on most bands. 30 m is 200 W PEP. 60 m is 100 W ERP. Technician HF privileges cap at 200 W PEP.
Drive
Match the amp to your exciter. Most legal-limit amps want 50 to 100 W. QRP rigs need a small amp like the Hardrock-50.
Keying
Old tube rigs and amps can switch high relay voltage. Use a keying interface before connecting to a modern radio.
RF exposure
FCC rules require a routine RF exposure evaluation of your station. Run it again when you add an amp.
Power
Legal-limit amps want a 240 V circuit. A 120 V 15 A outlet tops out around 800 to 1,000 W out.
Tuner
Your tuner must handle the amp's output. A 100 W internal ATU goes between radio and amp only if the amp is bypassed.

Hardware that helps

Raspberry Pi digital station

A low-power FT8, Winlink and APRS station.

  1. Hardware. Pi 4 (4 GB) or Pi 5, a good 5 V supply, SSD over USB rather than an SD card for logging, and a DigiRig or the radio's own USB audio.
  2. Time. FT8 needs a clock within about one second. Install chrony; add a USB GPS for field use with no internet.
  3. Rig control. Install Hamlib (rigctld) or flrig so every program shares the same CAT connection.
  4. Modes. WSJT-X for FT8/FT4/WSPR, JS8Call for keyboard messaging, fldigi for PSK and RTTY, Direwolf for APRS and packet.
  5. Map and log. GridTracker reads WSJT-X traffic and maps it. Log to QLog or CQRLog, upload to LoTW with TQSL.
  6. Remote. Run wfview (Icom) or VNC over a home VPN so you can operate from another room or another town.

Interface cheat sheet

USB built in
IC-7300, IC-705, IC-9700, FT-991A, FT-710, FTDX10, TS-590SG, TS-890S. One cable carries audio and CAT.
CAT only
FT-891, FT-857D, FT-817/818. Add a DigiRig or SignaLink for audio.
No CAT
Older rigs and most HTs. Use VOX, or a SignaLink, and set frequency by hand.
Levels
Set transmit audio so ALC stays near zero. Overdriving splatters FT8 across the band.
RF in USB
Snap-on ferrites on USB and audio cables fix most lockups when transmitting.
Hall 7 · WebSDR

Listen from anywhere

Receivers around the world that anyone can tune in a browser. Use them to hear your own signal from another continent, check whether a band is open, or listen with no antenna at all. These need internet; they open in a new tab.

Hear yourself on the air

  1. Open a WebSDR or KiwiSDR 500 to 3,000 miles away. Too close and you're inside the skip zone.
  2. Tune it to a clear frequency in your band and set the right mode (LSB below 10 MHz, USB above).
  3. Turn your local audio down or use headphones so you don't feed back, then call. Expect a one to two second delay.
  4. On FT8 or CW you don't need a listener at all: check PSKReporter or the Reverse Beacon Network to see who decoded you.
