How the ratings work

Purchase cost runs from $ (under $6k typical) to $$$$$ (over $18k), based on the middle of the typical private-party range for the years listed. Prices vary a lot by mileage, rust and region.

5-yr cost = repairs × 5 + fuel for 5 years + expected value lost. Insurance, tax and registration are left out since they depend on you, not the car. The grade on the yellow sticker ranks each car's 5-yr cost against the others on this list: A is the cheapest fifth, F the priciest.

10-yr cost uses the same pieces over ten years, with repairs in years 6–10 bumped up 50% because an aging car needs more work. Use Grade by to grade on the 5- or 10-year number.

Parts & repairs only leaves fuel out of every total and grades cars on repair dollars alone, adding 15% for each point the Parts rating falls below 5, so a cheap-to-fix car with parts on every shelf scores best. The Prius 10-year cost includes a $2,500 hybrid battery replacement.

Lasts to is the mileage well-maintained examples commonly reach before a major failure makes them not worth fixing. Cars at 250,000 or more get the 250K+ club tag, and the 250K+ only button filters to just those.

Repairs/yr are typical averages for the model (unscheduled repairs plus routine maintenance), in the style of RepairPal's figures. The HHR's $542 matches RepairPal's current number; the rest are estimates and older, high-mileage examples will run higher.

Check a listing estimates a fair price from where the year falls in the guide's range and how the miles compare to the car's typical life. It's a quick sanity check, not a substitute for local listings or a pre-purchase inspection.

Part prices, maintenance, snow and tow figures are typical values from general knowledge, not looked up per car. Confirm fluid types and capacities in the owner's manual for the exact engine.
