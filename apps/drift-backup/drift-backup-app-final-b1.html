What people actually hate about backup software

Not features. Fears.

Nobody wakes up wanting a backup app. They want to never think about it — and to trust it completely on the one day they need it.

"Is it even working?"Backup software fails silently for weeks. You find out the day you need a file back.
Drift shows its workA quiet weekly note: what changed, what's protected, and a plain-language confidence score — not a log file nobody reads.
"Why is my laptop crawling right now?"Backups kick off on a timer, right when you sit down to actually work.
Drift reads the roomIt only moves during real idle — mouse still, fans quiet, battery not a concern — and steps aside instantly the moment you touch a key.
"I'll deal with restore settings later."Nobody tests a restore until disaster day — and that's when they discover the backup was incomplete.
Drift tests itselfOnce a week, it quietly restores a handful of random files to a hidden folder and checks they open. You never lift a finger.
"Wait — is this a ransomware attack?"If something starts mass-encrypting your files, most backup tools just... back up the encrypted mess too.
Drift notices the patternA sudden wave of file rewrites pauses backup automatically and flags it, so your last clean version stays safe.
