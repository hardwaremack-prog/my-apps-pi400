              What to gear up with

              • Hand-crank or battery AM/FM + NOAA weather radio. Your news feed. Listening needs no license.
              • FRS walkie-talkies. No license, short range, good for the household and next-door.
              • GMRS radios. More range; needs an FCC license (one covers the family, no test).
              • Ham radio. Town-to-town and farther; needs a license and a test. A great skill to learn now.
              • Paper. A neighborhood message board at a corner or the mailbox cluster works when nothing else does.

              Morse code

              Type a message to see it in Morse, and play it to learn it by sound. Send it by flashlight, whistle or radio. More in the Guide: "Morse code & improvised signaling" and "Antennas you can build."

              Listening practice

              Hear a random letter or word, then type what you heard. Start with the easy letters and slow speed.

              Listen and decode

              Turns Morse code heard through the microphone into letters: a radio's speaker, a whistle, a buzzer or the Play button on another device. Works best with a clear, steady tone and little background noise. The first few letters may come out wrong while it learns the sending speed.

              Wire antenna calculator

              Pick a band and antenna type to get cutting lengths. Transmit only where you're licensed or license-free (CB, FRS, GMRS and MURS have their own rules). Details in the Guide: "Antennas you can build."

              Trimming helper

              Digital mode monitor (PSK31)

              Decodes PSK31 text heard from a radio. Hold the radio's speaker near the microphone, or better, run a cable from its headphone jack to the mic input. Click a signal on the waterfall to tune it. Listening and decoding is legal for anyone.

              Band presets

              Tap a frequency to see how to tune the radio for it. All are USB dial frequencies in MHz.

              Send (type and play)

              Type a message, or tap a macro, then press Send. Rugged plays it as PSK31 through the speakers on the tuned frequency. Practice by playing it from one device and decoding on another. Sending over the air needs a ham license: PSK31 on HF needs a General license, and Technicians can use 10 meters (28.0–28.3 MHz) and VHF/UHF.

                New school

                Kids still need school, and adults need new skills. Learn by doing the real work: every chore is a lesson. One a day.

                Scout ranks & merit badges

                Scouts BSA is Scouting America's program for ages 11–17, open to boys and girls. Scouts move up through 7 ranks and can earn about 140 merit badges, each one a real skill. To earn a badge: pick one, talk with your Scoutmaster, get a merit badge counselor, do the requirements, and have them signed off. Many badges teach the exact skills in this app.
